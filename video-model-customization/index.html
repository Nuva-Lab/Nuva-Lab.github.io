Why customize

A general model is not a production specification

A strong base video model may still miss the details that determine whether a clip ships: a recurring character changes between shots, a product drifts from its approved design, a campaign loses its visual language, or a short-drama pipeline spends more time regenerating than producing.

Our video model fine-tuning service begins with those failures, not with a predetermined training recipe. We freeze representative prompts, references, formats, durations, and acceptance decisions; establish a reproducible baseline; then select the smallest model, data, runtime, or workflow change that can close the measured gap.

Customization scope

Distillation and acceleration

Train and qualify a reduced-step or sparse production path when latency, throughput, or hardware economics require changes that runtime tuning alone cannot provide.

Accepted-output evaluation

Optimize for the clip that ships

A faster generation is not a production improvement when it creates more unusable outputs. We compare the baseline and customized profile on the same workload, model settings, reference bundle, delivery format, and review rubric.

The evaluation can track first-pass acceptance, attempts per accepted clip, character and brand fidelity, temporal defects, safety failures, P50 and P95 completion time, GPU memory, complete-media latency, and cost per accepted second. Results remain separated by model profile and hardware topology so an adapter gain is not confused with a serving gain.

Decision gateA customization moves forward only when it delivers a material quality, consistency, speed, privacy, control, or accepted-output cost advantage on the agreed workload.

Deployment artifact

The deliverable is more than a weight file

A production handoff can include the agreed adapter or checkpoint, model and inference configuration, container or machine image, dependency lock, API contract, evaluation pack, reference-conditioning policy, monitoring thresholds, rollback artifact, and runbook.

For dedicated hardware, we also record the target GPU topology, precision and kernel path, memory envelope, supported output profiles, throughput and latency receipts, failure behavior, and the exact version relationship between training and serving.

This makes the customized model reproducible, reviewable, and portable within the rights and environments agreed for the project.

Rights and ownership

Define every layer before training starts

Open-weight does not automatically mean unrestricted, open source, or approved for every commercial deployment. Upstream model weights, code, and third-party components remain subject to their respective licenses. Customer-provided footage, characters, brands, product images, voices, and other references must be authorized for the intended processing and use.

Nuva Lab keeps upstream assets, customer inputs, training data, evaluation records, adapters, full checkpoints, runtime code, and deployment artifacts distinct. Ownership, access, retention, permitted reuse, export, and deletion terms are documented in the engagement agreement; we do not present upstream models or customer IP as Nuva-owned technology.

FAQ

Custom video model services

What is included in a video model fine-tuning service?

A Nuva Lab engagement begins with workload, data-rights, and baseline evaluation. Depending on the measured gap, the work may include dataset and conditioning design, LoRA or adapter training, broader post-training, distillation, inference optimization, accepted-output evaluation, and a versioned deployment artifact.

Can you improve character, product, or brand consistency?

Yes, when the customer has the necessary rights to the reference assets and the selected model supports the required conditioning or adaptation path. Nuva Lab measures identity, wardrobe, product, logo, palette, shot, and motion consistency against a frozen customer workload rather than promising perfect consistency from a single technique.

Do we need a LoRA, a fine-tuned checkpoint, or a distilled model?

Not always. LoRA can be useful for a bounded style, subject, or behavior shift; broader post-training may be needed for a larger distribution change; and distillation is appropriate when latency or throughput requires a trained few-step model. Nuva Lab chooses the smallest intervention that passes the agreed evaluation.

Who owns the customized model and training data?

Upstream model weights remain governed by their original licenses, and customer-provided data remains subject to the customer's rights and the engagement terms. Ownership and permitted use of adapters, checkpoints, datasets, evaluation packs, and deployment code are defined in the project agreement before training begins.

What is delivered at the end of the project?

The agreed delivery can include a versioned adapter or checkpoint, inference configuration, container or machine image, API contract, evaluation report, rights and provenance manifest, operating runbook, rollback artifact, and deployment receipt for the target hardware.
