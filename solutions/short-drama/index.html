The series problem

A viral shot is not an episodic production system

Short drama compounds the hardest parts of video generation. The audience must recognize the same characters and relationships across scenes, while the production team ships many vertical episodes under tight schedules and platform-specific delivery rules.

A model can produce an impressive isolated clip and still fail the series. Identity drift, wardrobe changes, spatial discontinuity, broken dialogue timing, inconsistent pacing, and repeated regeneration can erase the speed advantage. A dedicated system must optimize the accepted episode, not a cherry-picked generation.

Why dedicated capacity

Protect the schedule from the regeneration curve

Short-drama workloads can be both high volume and highly repetitive, which makes them promising candidates for model adaptation and reserved infrastructure. They are also unforgiving: a small drop in character or narrative acceptance can multiply retries across an entire episode.

We benchmark candidate systems against the current workflow using representative dialogue, action, establishing, reaction, and transition shots. Quality is reviewed first. Capacity and cost are then calculated from complete media delivery, actual attempts per accepted shot, operator time, peak episode schedule, and the hardware utilization the team can realistically sustain.

Qualification gateA private system should ship only when it meets the series quality bar and demonstrates a meaningful advantage in accepted throughput, continuity control, private asset handling, regional deployment, or cost per accepted episode minute.

Model choice and rights

Choose for the series, license for the territory

MiniMax H3 and FastH3 may be evaluated for synchronized video-and-audio generation, while other open-weight models may be stronger for particular visual styles, reference modes, regions, or commercial terms. FastVideo is an independent Apache-2.0 open-source framework used for post-training and inference; that framework license does not grant rights to every model it can run.

Commercial deployment is conditioned on the selected model's license and written authorization, derivative terms, deployment territory, source and reference rights, talent and music rights, and the customer's intended distribution. Nuva Lab keeps the serving and evaluation system portable so model supply can change without rebuilding the entire production line.
